# Garrick's Inn / Starting Area

## Ownership / scope

This document is the continuity home for the **Inn / Starting Area** sub-chat.

The sub-chat owns Inn-facing design such as:
- first-floor room layout and sightlines
- interactable objects and ambient details
- NPC placement inside the inn
- Garrick/Marlow/Sylvie reactive dialogue that occurs in the inn
- kitchen, basement, upstairs, bar/shop, contract-board, and room-rental presentation
- relationship-driven access changes inside the building
- secrets, flavor interactions, and future Inn expansion hooks

It does **not** override Central Brain or upstream system authorities. Hunting mechanics belong in `SYSTEMS_HUNTING.md`; Cooking mechanics belong in `SYSTEMS_COOKING.md`; opening chronology belongs in `OPENING_FLOW.md`. If Inn design discovers a contradiction with upstream canon, flag it for Central Brain instead of silently rewriting it.

Garrick's Inn is the player's first home base and should feel like a believable building before it feels like a menu hub.

## Main floor

Important spaces:
- front entrance
- Garrick's bar
- Hunting/contract board behind/near Garrick's bar
- common-room tables
- Marlow's initial table near enough to participate in the opening scene
- kitchen door
- stairs to rentable rooms
- basement/lab access
- fireplace / warm social area

The player should understand most of these visually without labels or tutorial callouts.

## Initial autonomy

The player gets control immediately after character creation.

Contextual reactions replace invisible tutorial walls:

### Kitchen before introduction
Garrick stops the stranger. He can joke that he does not even know their name yet and they are already trying to enter his kitchen. This should naturally roll into Garrick's introduction rather than creating an infinitely repeatable bark.

### Upstairs before introduction
Garrick explains that rooms cost money. This establishes an early coin goal and can point toward work.

### Basement before invitation
Marlow, still seated upstairs, can alert Garrick that someone seems to be entering his laboratory. Garrick intervenes and the interaction converges into his introduction.

### Contract board before trust
Player can inspect enough to understand that work exists. Trying to claim a posting causes Garrick to intervene because he does not yet know whether this stranger is capable.

### Merchandise
Looking is fine. Theft produces warnings and can escalate.

### Leaving immediately
Allowed. Garrick may make one contextual remark. If the player keeps walking, they genuinely leave. The opening waits for their return and Garrick remembers the behavior.

### Idling
Allowed. Garrick may eventually acknowledge the new face without forcing control.

## Garrick's bar

Garrick sells basic weapons, armor, drinks, and rooms, and acts as the informal conduit for local work.

The contract board is physically mounted behind/near his bar so taking jobs naturally involves him.

Garrick is not an official guildmaster. Locals trust that adventurers and capable travelers pass through his inn, so they leave problems with him.

## Marlow's table

Marlow initially sits in the common room because he is eating/taking a break. His table can contain:
- half-eaten meal
- drink
- notebook
- satchel
- harmless specimens/herbs
- a small amount of alchemical clutter because he cannot fully stop working

The accidental breakage of his last usable research sample occurs during Garrick's introduction.

## Basement laboratory

Marlow's lab should contrast with the warm common room:
- stone / cellar construction
- shelves of ingredients
- drying herbs
- expedition equipment
- creature sketches
- specimens
- workbench
- alchemy apparatus
- creature habitats
- brewing/fermentation equipment connected to the Garrick ale arrangement

The lab should contain future hooks that are simply part of the environment, not CONTENT COMING SOON signs.

## Kitchen

Sylvie's kitchen is socially private even though Garrick owns the inn.

Garrick's established philosophy:

> **"My inn. Her kitchen."**

The kitchen becomes accessible by explicit invitation after the first completed Hunting contract.

Early features:
- stove/heat source
- preparation table
- knives/tools
- pans/cookware
- pantry
- bowls/prep vessels

Cooking MVP operates through physical/contextual kitchen interactions rather than a floating craft menu. The authoritative Cooking grammar lives in `SYSTEMS_COOKING.md`.

# Sylvie's first kitchen sequence

## Shared entrance beat

After the player's first Hunting contract, Garrick notices the edible material and sends the player through to Sylvie.

Preferred Garrick line:

> **Garrick:** "Take it through to her. I'd call her out, but I've survived this long by remembering one thing."
>
> **Garrick:** "My inn. Her kitchen."

The player enters while Sylvie is already working.

> **Sylvie:** "Out."

> **Player:** "Garrick sent me."

Sylvie is ready to object to Garrick sending a stranger into her kitchen, then notices what the player is carrying. Her attention shifts immediately from territorial annoyance to professional interest.

Sylvie is good enough to identify the **exact animal/material immediately**, not merely the broad type. Depending on the material, she can often infer age, diet, condition, freshness, preservation state, harvest quality, and ideal preparation.

Her knowledge is practical culinary mastery. Marlow may know why an animal's biology works a certain way; Sylvie knows what that biology means when deciding how to prepare it.

## Variant A: Mud in the Moonrice

### Kill route: Fresh Reedback Haunch

Sylvie recognizes it immediately:

> **Sylvie:** "Reedback."

She examines the flesh and fat.

> **Sylvie:** "Fresh. Reedwater animal, too. Been feeding on mudgrubs."

This quietly confirms the player's Hunting inference that the Reedback was digging for mudgrubs rather than deliberately eating the rice.

If the player confirms they killed it:

> **Sylvie:** "Then use it."
>
> **Sylvie:** "You took a life to get it. Don't waste what it gave you."

This is responsibility, not moral condemnation.

**First dish:** a simple seared Reedback preparation with a restrained local herb finish.

**Demonstrated Cooking beats:**
- inspect grain and freshness
- slice across the grain
- season appropriately
- bring pan to proper high heat without scorching
- sear with minimal handling
- read browning/sizzle
- turn once appropriate
- remove from heat
- rest before slicing

Useful Sylvie beat:

> **Player:** "It's done."
>
> **Sylvie:** "Cooking's done. Food isn't."

The system-level lesson is **structure matters**.

### Redirect route: Preserved Reedback Cut

Sylvie immediately recognizes both species and condition:

> **Sylvie:** "Reedback. Preserved. Salt-cured. Few weeks, maybe."

If the player says Toma Reed supplied it:

> **Sylvie:** "Toma Reed?"

If local continuity supports Sylvie knowing his preserving habits:

> **Sylvie:** "I know his salt."

When the player explains the current Reedback was redirected and the paddies are safe:

> **Sylvie:** "And the rice?"
>
> **Player:** "Safe."
>
> **Sylvie:** "Good."

No pacifism speech is needed. The solution worked, the animal survived, and an older cull still provides usable food.

**First dish:** crisped preserved Reedback slices with a fresh balancing finish.

**Demonstrated Cooking beats:**
- inspect preserved state
- recognize existing salt and reduced moisture
- slice thinner than fresh meat
- avoid unnecessary extra salt
- use moderate heat
- render/crisp rather than hard-sear
- taste/assess and balance at the finish

The system-level lesson is **condition matters**.

## Variant B: Three Missing by Morning

### Guaranteed input: Fresh Duskhen Eggs

Sylvie sees the eggs:

> **Sylvie:** "Duskhen."

She checks one against the light or otherwise inspects it without cracking it.

> **Sylvie:** "Fresh."

If the player asks how she can tell:

> **Sylvie:** "Yes."

The joke is that the player asked whether she could tell, not how.

The ingredient itself does **not** reveal whether the player killed the Nightquill or secured/deterred its hunting route. Sylvie only reacts to that choice if the player tells her what happened.

If the Nightquill was killed:

> **Sylvie:** "Killed it?"

After confirmation:

> **Sylvie:** "Then don't leave what you took unused."

Do not imply extra Nightquill materials unless upstream content later establishes them.

If the hunting route was secured non-lethally:

> **Player:** "Blocked its glide route. Can't get through the roof anymore."
>
> **Sylvie:** "Birds are safe?"
>
> **Player:** "Yeah."
>
> **Sylvie:** "Then you solved it."

Sylvie does not rank the route morally. She cares that the problem was solved responsibly.

**First dish:** soft-folded Duskhen eggs.

This is intentionally humble food. Sylvie demonstrates mastery by making something extraordinary from a simple ingredient.

**Demonstrated Cooking beats:**
- crack and inspect
- mix/fold to the right texture
- use gentle heat
- keep the eggs moving
- read visual setting rather than a timer
- remove slightly loose
- let residual heat finish the dish

Useful exchange:

> **Player:** "They're eggs."
>
> **Sylvie:** "Very observant."

The system-level lesson is **control matters**.

## Variant C: When the Wheel Stopped

### Kill route: Fresh harvested Brookmaw Tail

Sylvie recognizes it immediately:

> **Sylvie:** "Brookmaw tail."

She checks the base and flesh.

> **Sylvie:** "Harvested fresh."

If asked how she knows, she can point out the cut, fresh blood/flesh, and handling condition rather than treating the recognition as magic.

After learning the player cleared the Brookmaws lethally, Sylvie's stance is again practical:

> **Sylvie:** "Then we're using this."

**First dish:** pan-crisped Brookmaw tail.

**Demonstrated Cooking beats:**
- inspect anatomy
- trim appropriately
- score through skin/fat without cutting deeply into flesh
- begin at moderate heat to render
- read fat/skin changes
- increase heat near the finish
- crisp exterior
- remove and plate

Useful correction:

> **Sylvie:** "Through the skin. Not through the flesh."

The system-level lesson is **anatomy matters**.

### Relocation route: Naturally Shed Brookmaw Tail

Sylvie sees it:

> **Sylvie:** "Brookmaw tail."

She checks the base.

> **Sylvie:** "Shed."

If the player asks how she can tell:

> **Sylvie:** "Clean separation. No cut. No tearing. Recent, too."

When the player explains they relocated/restored the nursery and found the tail already caught in debris:

> **Sylvie:** "Good."

She knows Brookmaws naturally regrow their paddle-shaped tails.

The naturally shed material should not be treated as a consolation ingredient. Its source and handling simply demand different preparation.

**First dish:** the same broad crisped Brookmaw-tail family, adapted to the shed material.

**Demonstrated Cooking beats:**
- inspect freshness/source
- clean carefully
- trim if needed
- dry thoroughly before cooking
- score
- render
- increase heat
- crisp

The system-level lesson is **source and handling matter**.

## Shared tasting beat

Whichever variant occurred, Sylvie plates an intentionally simple dish and the player tastes it.

Preferred universal exchange:

> **Player:** "...What did you do to this?"
>
> **Sylvie:** "Cooked it."
>
> **Player:** "No. Seriously."
>
> **Sylvie:** "So am I."

Ingredient-specific alternates may include:

**Reedback**

> **Player:** "How is this just meat?"
>
> **Sylvie:** "It isn't 'just' anything."

**Duskhen Eggs**

> **Player:** "They're eggs."
>
> **Sylvie:** "I noticed."

**Brookmaw Tail**

> **Player:** "I found this stuck in a mill."
>
> **Sylvie:** "And?"

Sylvie is not bashful or surprised by praise. She already knows she is exceptional.

If asked for the secret, the direction remains:

> **Sylvie:** "There isn't one. Good ingredient. Right preparation. Right pan. Right heat. Right amount of time."

Exact phrasing can be polished in implementation.

## Pantry arrangement and exact Cooking unlock

After tasting the dish, the player can ask Sylvie to teach them.

The structural sequence is locked:

**Sylvie prepares first dish → player tastes it → player asks to learn → Sylvie explains the arrangement → player accepts → Cooking unlocks.**

Cooking unlocking means **Sylvie has agreed to teach the player**, not that the player became competent simply by watching once.

Preferred arrangement language:

> **Sylvie:** "Bring enough to cook with."
>
> **Sylvie:** "And enough that I'm not teaching you at everyone else's expense."

The player contributes raw ingredients to Sylvie's pantry rather than novice-cooked meals.

If the conversation reaches why she feeds people who cannot pay:

> **Player:** "Even if they can't pay?"
>
> **Sylvie:** "They're hungry, aren't they?"

At acceptance:

**COOKING UNLOCKED**

Sylvie can immediately undercut any sense of instant mastery:

> **Sylvie:** "Don't look so pleased."

She puts a knife onto the preparation table.

> **Sylvie:** "You haven't cooked anything yet."

If enough ingredient remains, she offers an immediate supervised attempt, but it is optional:

> **Sylvie:** "There's enough left if you want to try."

The player may cook now or leave. The kitchen remains available for later training.

## Beginner supervision

At low Cooking competence, Sylvie hovers and her corrections replace some conventional tutorial UI.

Examples:

> **Sylvie:** "Not that knife."

> **Sylvie:** "Thinner."

> **Sylvie:** "Heat's too high."

> **Sylvie:** "Stop touching it."

> **Sylvie:** "What did I just say?"

As trust and skill improve, her behavior should relax. A rare **"Good."** becomes meaningful. Eventually the relationship should be felt spatially and behaviorally, culminating in something as simple as:

> **Sylvie:** "Need the stove?"

This should contrast directly with her first **"Out."**

## Food identity

Food does **not** function as another healing potion.

Potions retain immediate/reactive uses such as healing. Food provides preparation-based, non-healing benefits. Exact Well Fed/rest interactions remain system-level and unresolved where noted in `SYSTEMS_COOKING.md`.

All five starter dish variants should provide mechanically equivalent introductory value so neither contract choice nor lethal/non-lethal resolution creates a hidden "bad tutorial reward."

## Upstairs / room rental

Room rental is Day One content.

The cheapest room should cost roughly **5–10 coins beyond starting wealth**. Marlow's reward should move the player closer; exploration can potentially let the player afford it sooner.

If the player can afford it early, let them rent it. Do not artificially preserve poverty to force the Hunting path.

Exact Rest Quality effects are unresolved.

Long-term room ideas may include storage, personalization, trophies, and better rest, but do not overbuild them until Rest/player housing is formally designed.

## Relationship-driven space changes

The same physical space should feel different as trust grows.

Examples:
- kitchen: initially blocked → Garrick later explicitly sends player inside → Sylvie eventually may trust player with independent access
- basement: initially private → Marlow invites player → lab becomes Potion Making home
- board: visible but gated by Garrick's trust → available after player demonstrates competence or sequence-breaks
- upstairs: initially a coin goal → potentially becomes player's first home

The inn should evolve from "place the tutorial happened" into a genuine recurring home base.
