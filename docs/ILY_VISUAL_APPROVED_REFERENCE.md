# Modeling-reference brief

## 1. Proportion targets

Use the approved working envelope:

- height: approximately **168–178 cm / 5'6"–5'10"**
- build: athletic, work-conditioned, compact rather than bodybuilder-stylized
- apparent age: mid-20s to mid-30s

Proportion direction:

- strong legs, back, hands and forearms from repetitive physical work
- practical shoulder width and torso mass without heroic armor proportions
- stable lower-body stance suited to mud, channels and uneven ground
- natural limb proportions that support bracing, crouching, lifting and long-reach spear work
- hands large/readable enough for convincing tool and spear grips

Avoid both fragile anime-doll proportions and exaggerated combat-athlete anatomy.

## 2. Silhouette-critical shapes

Must survive the move from key art into 3D:

- dark secured-up hair mass
- compact athletic body shape
- light rolled-sleeve work shirt
- dark reed/green scarf mass around shoulders/neck
- green/dark waist-wrap shape
- loose dark trousers
- distinct wrapped wet-ground lower-leg / boot mass
- clean long spear line extending well beyond the body

At gameplay distance, Ily should remain identifiable even before small tools, facial features or mud textures are visible.

Do not let secondary rope loops, wraps or tool pieces fragment the silhouette.

## 3. Face features that must survive 3D translation

Preserve the approved face identity rather than drifting toward generic realism or a generic anime heroine.

Must retain:

- confident, alert stylized-anime facial construction
- grounded, observant neutral expression
- capacity for dry amusement / restrained competitiveness without constant aggression
- age read appropriate to mid-20s through mid-30s
- capable working-person presence rather than polished glamour

3D translation should avoid:

- photoreal skin treatment
- overly youthful giant-eye proportions
- glamour makeup as the main face read
- permanently angry brows / battle expression
- hyper-sharp fantasy-warrior facial planes

Weather exposure may be communicated through restrained material/texture treatment without turning her face into gritty realism.

## 4. Hair features that must survive 3D translation

Hair is a major profession and silhouette cue.

Preserve:

- dark color family
- secured-up working state
- compact bun / tied-up mass consistent with the approved sheet
- a few controlled loose locks around the face for shape appeal
- stylized anime clump language rather than strand-by-strand realism

The hair must remain believable around water, mud, tools and physical labor.

Avoid long loose heroic hair that would interfere with work.

For modeling, prioritize a few strong sculpted masses, a readable tied-up silhouette and limited secondary locks.

## 5. Essential clothing layers

Model the outfit as a small number of clear functional layers:

1. light practical work shirt
2. dark reed/green scarf or neck/shoulder wrap
3. green/dark waist-wrap / field cloth
4. loose dark field trousers
5. lower-leg wraps / gaiter treatment
6. durable wet-ground work boots

Optional minor construction may include one compact belt/fastening layer only where needed to anchor rope or a local work tool.

The outfit must support:

- crouching
- bracing
- stepping through shallow water / mud
- bending over channels
- lifting and levering
- long-reach spear movement

Avoid decorative cape panels, armor plates or loose fantasy layers that fight work movement.

## 6. Essential props

Must-keep prop:

- **long simple field spear**

Contextual profession props:

- rope
- one compact repair wedge / ditch-gate tool
- one small hook / pry / cleaning tool or equivalent local implement

These should remain contextual and low-density.

The spear remains the only major permanent silhouette prop.

## 7. Spear dimensions and gameplay readability

Exact combat-production dimensions are not locked here, but use this as the modeling starting envelope:

- overall length roughly **1.15–1.30× Ily's body height**
- long enough that both spearhead and butt/ferrule clearly extend outside her body silhouette in neutral and ready poses
- shaft slim enough to read as a practical spear rather than a staff, but thick enough to remain visible at gameplay distance
- head compact, plain and forged rather than oversized
- butt/ferrule visibly reinforced without becoming decorative

Final exact length may be tuned later for animation, collision, camera readability and approved combat reach, but shortening it must not erase the established long-reach identity.

Preserve:

- straight durable wooden shaft
- plain forged spearhead
- minimal practical shaft wrapping only where useful
- reinforced butt/ferrule

Avoid:

- winged blades
- gemstones
- runes/glow
- ceremonial tassels
- ornate crossguards
- oversized fantasy blade mass

## 8. Materials and palette

Primary read:

- light warm-neutral work shirt
- dark charcoal/brown trousers
- reed / muted forest-green scarf and waist-wrap
- dark wet leather / wrapped lower-leg values
- natural wood spear shaft
- dark iron / steel spear hardware

Material goal remains:

**stylized dark-fantasy anime key art translated into 3D**

Use controlled material distinction between cloth, wrapped fabric, leather, wet/muddy surfaces, wood and forged metal without chasing photoreal micro-detail.

Mud/water response should support the work story rather than become a global grunge pass.

## 9. Safe simplifications for 3D

Exact reproduction is **not** required for:

- every cloth wrap
- exact mud splatter
- tiny stitching
- minor rope loops
- tiny belt ties
- small tool geometry
- exact material / shader values
- microscopic repair marks
- exact fold placement

Safe simplification methods include:

- combining several wrap turns into one readable wrapped form
- baking mud/wear into material masks rather than separate geometry
- reducing rope coils to one clean prop shape
- collapsing small repair tools into one or two readable silhouettes
- simplifying spearhead facets while preserving its plain forged shape

## 10. Do not simplify away

Do not lose:

- Ily's approved face identity
- dark secured-up hair silhouette
- athletic work-conditioned proportions
- light shirt + dark/green layered color structure
- scarf / waist-wrap identity
- wet-ground lower-leg treatment
- practical boots
- worker-first posture
- long simple spear silhouette
- plain forged spearhead
- reinforced butt/ferrule
- low equipment density

These are the visual spine of the character.

## 11. Gameplay-distance priorities

From normal gameplay camera distance, priority order is:

1. worker-first athletic silhouette
2. long spear line
3. light shirt against dark/green layers
4. secured dark hair mass
5. wrapped / muddy lower-leg and boot treatment
6. rope or one compact local-tool cue only when visible

Tiny mud flecks, stitching and repair-tool details should disappear gracefully rather than become visual static.

The player should recognize Ily by **worker silhouette + spear + green/light/dark structure + wet-ground lower-body treatment**, not by reading accessories.

## 12. Animation / rigging considerations for later production

Prepare the eventual model so it can support:

- relaxed standing
- carrying / planting the spear
- probing ground or water depth
- checking channel flow
- crouching and kneeling
- bracing on unstable footing
- levering debris
- two-handed spear ready stance
- reach attacks / lane-control movements
- climbing over low banks / bunds

Keep waist-wrap and scarf construction animation-friendly.

Avoid long loose cloth requiring heavy simulation for routine gameplay.

The spear should have clear hand-spacing zones for work poses and combat poses without ornamental geometry obstructing grip changes.
