# Player Character Visual Direction

## Status

**PROPOSED PLAYER CHARACTER VISUAL DIRECTION — NOT YET A LOCKED CHARACTER REFERENCE.**

Central Brain approved documenting this direction so implementation and visual-development work can see where the player avatar is headed without mistaking an unfinished concept for canon.

This document does not authorize final character modeling, rigging, or replacement of working Unity assets by itself.

Project-wide visual style remains:

> **stylized dark-fantasy anime key art translated into 3D**

## Core identity

The player character should read as a **grounded young traveler with quiet hero energy and room to become whatever kind of adventurer the player shapes him into**.

He should not begin the game looking like:
- a chosen-one prince;
- an established champion;
- a royal knight;
- an armor-heavy mercenary veteran;
- a generic blank mannequin;
- an exaggerated anime edgelord.

The starting visual should communicate that he is capable enough to survive, inexperienced enough to grow, and ordinary enough to belong naturally in Garrick's Inn and the surrounding world.

## Proposed physical direction

Working proposal:
- **male**;
- apparent age approximately **24–26**;
- approximate height **5'9"–5'11"**;
- **lean athletic build** rather than bulky musculature;
- lightly weathered / travel-conditioned appearance;
- **dark-brown medium-length messy/layered hair**;
- **amber-brown / warm-brown eyes**;
- lightly tanned skin direction;
- attractive but grounded stylized-anime facial language.

These exact identity fields remain **proposed** until a player-character visual exploration/reference is reviewed and approved.

## Proposed outfit language

Starting outfit direction:
- short **blue-grey / slate-blue travel coat or layered tunic-jacket**;
- soft grey/cream undershirt;
- weathered brown leather belt/straps;
- small practical satchel or travel pack;
- dark durable trousers;
- practical dark boots;
- simple scarf / neck wrap;
- optional restrained wrist wraps or work gloves only if they remain functional;
- repaired/worn-but-maintained details rather than ornamental costume damage.

The outfit should support Sword, Spear, and Bow equipment without making one weapon the player's permanent visual identity.

Avoid:
- giant cape;
- ornate hero armor;
- royal heraldry;
- excessive pouches and straps;
- glowing chosen-one relic language;
- weapon-specific armor that makes other weapon choices visually awkward.

## Palette rationale

Primary working family:
- dusty blue / slate blue;
- charcoal;
- weathered leather brown;
- soft cream/grey;
- optional restrained faded-teal or steel-blue accent.

This helps the player remain visually distinct from the currently approved opening cast:
- Garrick's warm earth/oxblood proprietor language;
- Sylvie's white/dark/wine chef language;
- Marlow's olive/earth naturalist language;
- Ily's reed-green/wet-field worker language.

## Silhouette goal

At gameplay distance, the player should read as:

> **traveler / newcomer**

rather than noble, scholar, laborer, or innkeeper.

Useful silhouette anchors:
- medium-height upright body;
- short outer coat rather than long robe/cape;
- neck-wrap/scarf shape;
- one asymmetrical satchel strap;
- practical boots;
- currently equipped weapon visible without dominating the entire character.

## Narrative read

The starting design should quietly communicate:
- new to this place;
- capable;
- not wealthy;
- has spent time on the road;
- still has room to grow;
- does not already visually possess a fixed heroic destiny.

The player should sit visually between the more strongly occupation-coded opening cast members. This helps him function as the movable piece through which the player explores the world.

## Character model continuity — implementation requirement

Human playtest feedback found that the player character representation did not carry cleanly into at least one combat sequence.

Regardless of the final approved player appearance, implementation should preserve the same authoritative player appearance state across exploration and combat, including the equipped weapon and any supported equipment visuals.

Combat should not silently replace the current player appearance with a generic placeholder.

## Next visual-development step

When Character Visual Development is ready, create several exploratory player-character concepts within this direction before locking exact face/hair/outfit construction.

Do not treat this document's numerical ranges or exact identity fields as a final model sheet until Central Brain/project-owner review promotes an image/reference to approved status.
