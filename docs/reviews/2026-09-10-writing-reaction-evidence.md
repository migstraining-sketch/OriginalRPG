## What changes in the lens

The working interpretation is: **character appeal must be earned through motives, conduct, relationships and consequences; constant displays of cleverness cannot replace that work.** This is our synthesis, not a quotation.

The evidence increases confidence in scrutiny of forced coolness, inflated dialogue, references used as shortcuts and thin thematic writing. It also adds explicit counterexamples: he accepts some references, optional easy modes, political ideas handled well, and harmless animal interactions. Preserve those qualifications in future reviews.

Do not convert a comedic rant into demographic rules for writers, forbidden character appearances, a ban on warmth, or a requirement for every game to be grim. His entertaining stream delivery is not automatically a suitable register for our NPC dialogue. We are using expressed preferences to improve this game, not writing its cast as copies of a streamer.

## Application to WoodlandSpine

These are review judgments against the character and dialogue authorities checked at main f30189d. No dialogue was edited.

- **Garrick's Bottle-Brain nickname: keep, with contrast.** It can show familiar affection. Constant use while Marlow is caring for a deteriorating patient would cheapen that relationship. Existing canon already supplies a strong contrast: Garrick uses Marlow's real name after his departure. Preserve the meaning of that shift instead of adding another gag.
- **Marlow's troll scene: protect sincerity.** His observation about the missing green coloration works because he notices a concrete symptom. Show competent care, uncertainty and attention to the patient. Do not turn his awkwardness into professional incompetence or append a self-aware joke to every vulnerable moment.
- **The treatment payoff: let it land.** A change in appetite/color and Marlow's relief provide the reward. Test the scene with room for the player to observe; do not immediately cover the result with banter or an unlock explanation.
- **Garrick's kitchen line: keep if the staging earns it.** The established distinction between his inn and Sylvie's kitchen expresses a real relationship and territorial boundary. It does more than advertise wit. Repeating it every visit would make it a catchphrase tax.
- **Sylvie's bluntness: allow development.** Her first dismissal can establish a boundary. Continued contempt after the player earns trust would contradict relationship progression. Let changed conduct carry that progression instead of repeatedly assuring the player she secretly cares.
- **Ily, Sable and Nessa: differentiate investment, not just vocabulary.** Ily and Nessa already share practical repair-minded traits; Garrick and Sylvie also use dry speech. Give them distinct stakes, assumptions and responses under pressure. A name-hidden sample should often suggest a speaker through viewpoint, not just a trademark word. This is a diagnostic, not a rule that every ordinary sentence must be uniquely identifiable.
- **Cull versus Manage: keep practical consequences.** A credible disagreement can exist without labelling one route virtuous or making an NPC a mouthpiece. Show crops, habitat, labor and creature outcomes. The player should be allowed to care, disagree or prioritize differently.
- **Optional ecology: keep room for wonder.** The forager and spring need not justify themselves with cynicism or jokes. A character can be interested in something without mocking their own interest.

## Writing checks to apply from now on

1. What does the speaker want here, and what does the player need to understand?
2. Does the line establish character, context, action, a choice or a consequence? Atmosphere can count; filler cannot claim that automatically.
3. Is humor arising from these people and circumstances, or could it be pasted into almost any game?
4. Does the next joke erase a moment of fear, grief, affection, relief or wonder before it lands?
5. Are we asking the player to admire/care about someone before showing a reason?
6. Does the character actually believe in something, or only comment ironically on everything?
7. Could editing improve pace while preserving the setup needed to understand the payoff?
8. Are the player's responses plausible at this point, including sincere interest and disagreement?

Judge the complete scene, not isolated lines. Avoid a fixed jokes-per-minute quota, arbitrary word limit, automatic ban on colloquial speech, or mandatory tragic backstories.
