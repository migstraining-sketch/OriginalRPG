# Modeling-reference brief

## 1. Proportion targets

Use the approved working envelope:

- height: approximately **163-173 cm / 5'4"-5'8"**
- build: compact feminine, practical and work-capable
- apparent age: late 20s to early 40s

Proportion direction:

- compact overall mass rather than tall heroic proportions
- clear feminine silhouette without exaggerated pin-up anatomy
- shoulders and arms capable of believable lifting, chopping, stirring and carrying
- stable stance suitable for long hours on hard kitchen flooring
- natural leg proportions supporting quick movement around counters, ovens and tables

Avoid both fragile doll-like proportions and combat-hero exaggeration.

## 2. Silhouette-critical shapes

Must survive the jump from key art into 3D:

- short blonde hair mass
- compact upright head-chef stance
- white working layer framing the torso/arms
- dark apron as the dominant central clothing shape
- loose dark trouser silhouette
- wine-red sash as the clearest color accent
- sturdy compact dark work boots

The apron should read immediately from normal gameplay distance.

Do not let small hardware or ornament fragment the silhouette.

## 3. Face features that must survive 3D translation

Preserve the approved facial identity rather than drifting into generic realism or a different anime face.

Must retain:

- attractive stylized-anime facial construction
- blue eyes
- confident, composed neutral read
- ability to shift into focused, severe-evaluating, amused, or small-smile expressions
- mature competence without making her stern by default

3D translation should avoid:

- photoreal facial texturing that fights the project style
- overly juvenile proportions
- exaggerated glamour makeup
- permanent angry/scowling expression
- changing eye shape so far that she stops reading as the approved Sylvie

## 4. Hair features that must survive 3D translation

The short blonde hair is a primary identity cue.

Preserve:

- short length
- blonde family
- clean stylized anime clumps / masses
- soft asymmetry around face and crown
- enough strand grouping to feel lively without becoming loose food-safety nonsense
- recognizable silhouette from front, three-quarter and side views

Hair should remain controlled enough for kitchen work without forcing a bun or long tie-back that changes the approved identity.

For modeling, prioritize a few strong sculpted masses and readable secondary locks over strand-by-strand realism.

## 5. Essential clothing layers

Model the outfit as a small number of readable layers:

1. white practical chef base / working layer
2. dark apron as the dominant torso-to-knee profession layer
3. loose dark work trousers
4. wine / dark-red waist sash
5. sturdy dark kitchen boots

Optional restrained construction accents may include:

- apron strap hardware
- small collar/cuff structure
- one practical towel loop or attachment point

Avoid decorative complexity that adds rigging/simulation cost without strengthening identity.

The clothing must support:

- chopping and knife work
- stirring
- carrying pans and dishes
- bending and reaching
- plating
- moving between prep, hearth and service spaces

## 6. Essential props

Must-keep prop family:

- **chef knife** as primary signature work tool
- **kitchen towel / cloth** as secondary everyday tool

Contextual props may include:

- pan
- bowl
- ladle / spoon
- oven or heat gloves
- ingredients / serving dish

These are task props, not permanent costume attachments.

The chef knife should look exceptionally maintained, practical and professional rather than ceremonial or combat-oriented.

## 7. Footwear

Preserve sturdy closed dark footwear, but keep it firmly in **kitchen/work boot** territory.

Priorities:

- good floor grip
- toe/foot protection
- ankle stability
- compact readable shape
- easy movement on hard or potentially wet flooring

Avoid:

- armor plates
- decorative travel straps
- exaggerated fantasy soles
- knee-high adventurer silhouette
- excess buckles

The boots should disappear into the profession read rather than becoming a costume focal point.

## 8. Materials and color structure

Primary color/read family:

- white / warm off-white working layer
- dark charcoal / brown-black apron
- dark trousers
- wine / dark-red sash
- dark practical footwear
- blonde hair and blue eyes as character-identity contrast

Material goal:

**stylized dark-fantasy anime key art translated into 3D**

Use controlled distinction between:

- washable cloth
- heavier apron fabric
- restrained leather/metal hardware
- hair
- skin
- knife steel

Do not chase photoreal micro-surface detail.

Wear should be restrained and logical:

- faint apron wear or staining
- cuff/contact wear
- boot wear
- knife-handle wear

Sylvie should look actively working but meticulously maintained.

## 9. Safe simplifications for 3D

Exact reproduction is **not** required for:

- tiny embroidery
- individual seams and stitches
- minor buckles
- exact cloth folds
- exact shader/material values
- tiny decorative markings
- microscopic apron hardware

These may collapse into texture, normal, material, or simplified geometry treatment.

Small decorative details may be reduced further if they compete with readability, animation, or kitchen practicality.

## 10. Do not simplify away

Do not lose:

- Sylvie's approved face identity
- short blonde hair silhouette
- blue eyes
- compact feminine proportions
- confident head-chef posture
- white working layer
- dark apron mass
- loose dark trousers
- wine-red sash
- practical dark boots
- chef knife
- chef-first readability

These are the visual spine of the character.

## 11. Gameplay-distance readability

From normal gameplay camera distance, priority order is:

1. short blonde hair against darker Inn/kitchen values
2. compact upright silhouette
3. dark apron over white working layer
4. wine-red sash accent
5. chef knife or towel when contextually visible

Tiny embroidery, buckles and seams should disappear gracefully rather than turning into visual static.

The player should recognize Sylvie by **hair + apron silhouette + white/dark/red structure + posture**, not by reading miniature costume decoration.

## 12. Animation / rigging considerations for later production

Prepare the eventual model so it can support:

- relaxed standing
- hands-on-hips / evaluative posture
- chopping and slicing
- stirring
- tasting
- plating
- carrying pans / bowls / dishes
- reaching to shelves or hooks
- bending at prep surfaces
- oven / hearth handling with contextual gloves

Keep apron and sash construction animation-friendly.

Avoid long loose panels that require elaborate cloth simulation for ordinary kitchen movement.

Hands need enough deformation quality for convincing knife grip, food handling, plating and tasting gestures.
