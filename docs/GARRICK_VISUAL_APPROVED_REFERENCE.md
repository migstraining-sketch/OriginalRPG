# Modeling-reference brief

## 1. Proportion targets

Use the approved working envelope:

- height: approximately **188–193 cm / 6'2"–6'4"**
- apparent age: late 40s to mid-50s
- build: broad, heavy and physically capable

Proportion direction:

- broad shoulders without exaggerated heroic taper
- thick chest and substantial waist/core
- large forearms and hands
- sturdy legs capable of moving stock and barrels
- neck and upper back with enough mass to support the grounded silhouette
- practical human proportions rather than bodybuilder anatomy

The body should suggest strength built through years of work, lifting, repair, hauling, and occasional violence rather than gym-sculpted definition.

## 2. Silhouette-critical shapes

Must survive the move from key art into 3D:

- broad heavy torso mass
- medium rugged hair silhouette
- imperfect beard mass framing the lower face
- light rolled-sleeve shirt against darker vest/apron values
- large forearms and hands
- sturdy trouser volume
- heavy work-boot silhouette
- compact proprietor props such as keys or towel when contextually visible

At gameplay distance, Garrick should read as the largest and most grounded social anchor in the Inn without needing armor or a visible weapon.

Do not let small accessories fragment the silhouette.

## 3. Face features that must survive 3D translation

Preserve the approved face identity rather than drifting into a generic older anime warrior or direct Bobby Singer likeness.

Must retain:

- rugged stylized-anime facial construction
- mature late-40s to mid-50s read
- strong but not superhero-sharp facial planes
- tiredness that reads as lived experience rather than weakness
- capacity for dry amusement
- capacity for stern evaluation
- protective warmth when expression softens
- imperfect beard integrating naturally with jaw and cheeks

3D translation should avoid:

- photoreal skin aging
- exaggerated wrinkles
- direct celebrity likeness
- polished handsome-hero treatment
- permanent anger
- barbarian caricature

Use restrained texture, under-eye treatment, brow shape, beard breakup, and facial planes to carry age and history.

## 4. Hair and beard features that must survive 3D translation

Hair:

- medium length
- dark color family
- rugged and slightly messy
- low-maintenance working-man read
- stylized clumped forms rather than strand-by-strand realism
- avoid intentionally fashionable or salon-shaped styling

Beard:

- practical imperfect beard/stubble direction
- irregular enough to feel lived-in
- maintained enough not to read as neglect
- strong enough in silhouette to remain part of Garrick's identity

For modeling, prioritize a few readable hair masses and a beard shape that integrates with facial animation without becoming a rigid helmet.

## 5. Essential clothing layers

Model the default Inn outfit as a small number of clear functional layers:

1. sturdy light work shirt
2. dark practical vest
3. apron / waist work layer where needed
4. durable dark trousers
5. heavy practical work boots
6. restrained belt / proprietor attachment layer

The shirt should support:

- rolled sleeves
- lifting
- reaching shelves
- carrying barrels/crates
- wiping the bar
- repair work
- fighting without costume change

Avoid armor panels, decorative pauldrons, long heroic coat tails, or elaborate adventurer harnesses.

## 6. Essential props

Core contextual prop family:

- **Inn keys**
- **ledger**
- **towel / work cloth**
- **tankard / bar vessel**
- **simple utility knife**

Not all props need to be visible at once.

The keys and ledger are strong proprietor cues; the towel and tankard support bartender/Inn work; the utility knife supports practical competence.

No prop should overpower Garrick's body language or turn the silhouette into an inventory rack.

## 7. Outdoor-coat separation

The heavier outdoor coat is a **secondary situational garment**.

Use cases:

- cold weather
- supply runs
- outdoor repair
- travel

Model it separately from the default Inn outfit if production later requires it.

Do not make it a permanent layer in the main Garrick mesh unless there is a clear production reason.

Its silhouette may carry additional protective weight outdoors, but the default interior silhouette remains shirt + vest + practical work layers.

## 8. Materials and palette

Primary visual family:

- light warm-neutral shirt
- dark charcoal / worn-brown vest and work layers
- dark durable trousers
- deep brown leather
- muted warm / earth accents
- restrained dark red / oxblood accent
- dull iron / brass where hardware is needed

Material target remains:

**stylized dark-fantasy anime key art translated into 3D**

Use controlled distinction between cloth, leather, wood-adjacent wear, metal hardware, hair, beard and skin without chasing photoreal microdetail.

Wear should be strongest at:

- cuffs
- elbows
- apron/towel contact areas
- belt/key contact points
- boot edges
- frequently handled leather

## 9. Safe simplifications for 3D

Exact reproduction is **not** required for:

- tiny stitching
- apron ornament
- individual keys
- minor belt hardware
- exact cloth folds
- exact material / shader values
- microscopic stains
- minor seam placement
- tiny leather fasteners

Safe simplification includes:

- reducing a key ring to a few readable key silhouettes
- baking small wear into textures/material masks
- collapsing minor buckles into simpler hardware
- reducing apron/towel embroidery to material detail
- simplifying beard micro-strands into sculpted clumps/cards appropriate to the final pipeline

## 10. Do not simplify away

Do not lose:

- approved face identity
- rugged medium dark hair silhouette
- practical imperfect beard
- broad/heavy grounded body shape
- thick torso/core
- large forearms/hands
- light shirt / dark vest structure
- apron/towel work language
- durable dark trousers
- heavy work boots
- restrained proprietor props
- grounded working posture

These are Garrick's visual spine.

## 11. Gameplay-distance readability

From normal gameplay camera distance, priority order is:

1. broad/heavy grounded body mass
2. light shirt against dark vest/apron structure
3. rugged hair/beard silhouette
4. large forearms/hands and planted posture
5. keys / towel / ledger / tankard only when contextually visible
6. utility knife only at closer range or relevant interaction

Tiny stitching, keys, seam details and hardware should disappear gracefully rather than become visual static.

The player should recognize Garrick by **mass + shirt/vest/work layers + hair/beard + rooted proprietor posture**, not by a weapon.

## 12. Animation / rigging considerations for later production

Prepare the eventual model so it can support:

- relaxed behind-the-bar idle
- wiping / polishing surfaces
- handling tankards
- writing / checking ledger
- key handling
- lifting or rolling barrels/crates
- simple repair / tool-use gestures
- leaning on the bar
- arms-crossed evaluation
- stern / dryly amused conversational poses
- protective step-in / confrontation posture
- practical close-quarters fighting if required later

Hands and forearms need enough deformation quality for believable heavy-object handling and proprietor work.

Apron/towel construction should remain animation-friendly and avoid unnecessary cloth-simulation dependence.
